## Design process

Bruno's process generally follows: discover (user interviews, quantitative research,
workshops) → define & ideate (affinity diagrams, problem statements, hypotheses, user
flows) → design (information architecture, wireframing, visual design, interactive
prototypes) → output (usability testing, iterate after feedback, ship). See the
Jusbrasil case study for a concrete example.

Key principles:
- Always base decisions on user experiences, not opinions. What seems like a problem to
  you might not be one for users — validate first.
- Research depth depends on project constraints (time, budget, user access). If the
  project needs more info, always push for research to ship with a better foundation.
- When shipping something the team isn't fully confident about, document the assumptions
  so they can be reviewed against real results.
- Collaborate closely with the PM throughout — every decision is a conversation with
  consideration, not edict.

**Evolution & key learning:** Early in his career, Bruno was eager to jump to solutions
and ship quickly. Over time, he learned that taking time to gather information, analyze
it thoroughly, listen to different perspectives, and think through implications leads to
much better outcomes. Now he prioritizes that discovery and analysis phase before
planning and executing.

**Measuring success:** Success metrics depend on the project scope — could be user
retention, acquisition, user feedback, or a mix. Bruno works with the PM and stakeholders
to define what "success" looks like upfront, then measures against those signals
throughout and after launch.

**AI-assisted workflow:** Bruno sees AI as a tool that amplifies productivity and lets him
focus more on problem-solving, user needs, and the purpose behind a solution rather than
spending excessive time on repetitive execution.

His AI-integrated process:
1. **Discovery & planning:** Use AI to help define the challenge, map requirements, identify
   dependencies, explore user flows, and gather references. This creates a solid foundation
   before moving into solution design.
2. **Solution exploration:** With a well-structured Design System connected between Figma
   and code, use AI agents to explore multiple solutions based on the design documentation
   already built.
3. **Refinement:** Review the generated solutions. For small adjustments that feel faster by
   hand, move screens into Figma and refine directly. Then bring updated designs back into
   the AI workflow so the agent can continue building prototypes or additional screens based
   on the refined direction.
4. **Validation:** Create prototypes in a separate branch and present to stakeholders. Based
   on feedback, either use AI to iterate or make quick refinements directly in Figma if
   faster. Share updated prototypes for review again until aligned.
5. **Handoff & delivery:** Once approved, communicate specs with devs for handoff, do final
   validation, merge to main, and ship as part of the product.

The key is constant collaboration between human judgment and AI capability — using AI for
exploration and iteration speed, but maintaining creative control and strategic direction.

**Company size:** Both small and big companies work for him. Small companies let you be
a jack of all trades — build from scratch, touch all areas, own more of the product.
Bigger companies provide structure, processes, and resources to maximize productivity.
Both have trade-offs he appreciates.

**Type of work:** Passionate about creating experiences. Has worked across mobile apps,
design systems (his favorite), and web products. Loves owning the full design of a
product from concept to ship.

**Collaboration:** A natural collaborator. Has thrived as a solo designer (especially
with a product partner/CEO), but gets the best of himself working alongside other
designers — different perspectives and POVs make the work stronger. Values
cross-functional work with eng and product.

**Design systems:** Very passionate about this area. Has built or upgraded design systems
in most companies he's worked in. Loves the technical/systematic side — structuring
tokens, CSS variables, component specs, and maintaining rigor in Figma and code.

**Remote work:** Works well across timezones with good communication practices — regular
Slack updates, Loom recordings, and being hands-on with the work. Needs enough time to
be deeply involved in design solutions, not just high-level strategy.

**Growth:** Interested in learning new domains — particularly fintech. Values roles where
he can deepen his expertise in product design and design systems while also growing as a
designer and collaborator.

**Mentoring & leadership:** Has mentored other designers, but prefers being hands-on and
technical rather than people-focused. Enjoys being a technical reference and expert in a
company — helping other designers unblock, create better solutions, and level up their
craft. Not interested in people management, but very interested in elevating design
quality across the team.

**Handling disagreement:** Believes everything can be solved with good conversation.
Takes time to analyze different POVs, understand each perspective's strengths and
weaknesses, and stays flexible and open to different ideas. When he and a PM can't fully
align, they work together to find a sweet spot where the team can move forward with
confidence.
